Research Interests

Quantum matter, from first principles to the beamline

I work on both sides of the theory–experiment interface in condensed matter physics. As a theorist, I develop ab initio many-body frameworks — and the large-scale HPC pipelines behind them — that turn microscopic Hamiltonians into measurable observables: phonon linewidths, thermal conductivities, electron–phonon coupling matrix elements, and nonequilibrium quasiparticle distributions. As an experimentalist, I lead synchrotron and spectroscopy campaigns at national facilities (APS, CHESS, LANL), distilling IXS, ARPES, XRD, Raman, and transient-thermal-grating data into the same physical quantities — so prediction and measurement meet on common ground.

A unifying thread is coupled bosonic and fermionic excitations: how phonons scatter off charge-density-wave fluctuations, how magnons dress excitons in 2D magnets, and how a single theoretical language — Green's functions, Bethe–Salpeter and Kadanoff–Baym equations — can describe them together. This theory–experiment loop has produced multiple first-observation results in charge-density-wave and 2D magnetic materials, and on the applied side underpins my modeling of phonon-mediated quasiparticle poisoning in superconducting qubits.

01

Charge-density-wave physics

Microscopic theory of acoustic-phonon scattering by CDW fluctuations, and the weak first-order melting revealed by anomalous thermal transport in 2H-TaSe₂.

02

Electron–phonon coupling

GW-corrected, anisotropic, temperature-dependent coupling in Weyl semimetals and ultrawide-bandgap semiconductors — tied directly to spectroscopy.

03

Superconducting qubits

Nonequilibrium quasiparticle–phonon dynamics from first principles, toward an end-to-end model of qubit decoherence and poisoning.

04

Topology & 2D magnetism

Topological phonon–magnon hybrid excitations and magnetically dressed exciton-polaritons in van der Waals (anti)ferromagnets.

05

Synchrotron & ultrafast experiment

Leading IXS, ARPES, XRD, Raman, and transient-thermal-grating campaigns at APS, CHESS, and LANL — measuring phonon dispersions, linewidths, and order parameters to validate theory.
