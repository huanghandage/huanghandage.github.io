One predictive loop: quantum many-body theory to experiment to device, and back.

I work on both sides of the theory–experiment interface in condensed matter physics. As a theorist, I develop ab initio many-body frameworks (and the large-scale HPC pipelines behind them) that turn microscopic Hamiltonians into measurable observables: phonon linewidths, thermal conductivities, electron–phonon coupling matrix elements, and nonequilibrium quasiparticle distributions. As an experimentalist, I lead synchrotron and spectroscopy campaigns at national facilities (APS, CHESS, LANL), distilling IXS, XRD, and Raman data into the same physical quantities, so prediction and measurement meet on common ground.

A unifying thread is coupled bosonic and fermionic excitations: how phonons scatter off charge-density-wave fluctuations, how magnons dress excitons in 2D magnets, and how a single theoretical language (Green's functions, Bethe–Salpeter and Kadanoff–Baym equations) can describe them together. This theory–experiment loop has produced multiple first-observation results in charge-density-wave and 2D magnetic materials, and on the applied side underpins my modeling of phonon-mediated quasiparticle poisoning in superconducting qubits.

01

Exotic thermal properties in quantum materials

I study how quantum materials carry heat in unexpected ways, developing microscopic theory for anomalous, non-monotonic thermal transport. A central example is acoustic-phonon scattering by charge-density-wave (CDW) fluctuations: I construct the CDW propagator, vertex corrections, and phonon self-energy from first principles to explain the V-shaped thermal conductivity and weak first-order CDW melting observed in 2H-TaSe₂.

02

Electron–phonon coupling

I determine anisotropic, temperature-dependent electron–phonon coupling directly from spectroscopy combined with first-principles theory, including GW corrections that quantum-correct the bare coupling. This connects measured phonon linewidths and dispersions to microscopic coupling matrix elements in systems ranging from Weyl semimetals to ultrawide-bandgap semiconductors.

03

Superconducting qubits

I model nonequilibrium quasiparticle–phonon dynamics in superconductors from first principles, using Keldysh / Kadanoff–Baym formalism to follow how energy flows between Cooper pairs, quasiparticles, and phonons. The aim is an end-to-end predictive model of phonon-mediated quasiparticle poisoning (a leading decoherence channel in superconducting qubits), chaining energy deposition, phonon transport, and many-body dynamics.

04

Topology & 2D magnetism

I study topologically nontrivial bosonic excitations in two-dimensional magnets, where phonons and magnons hybridize to open Berry-curvature-carrying gaps in the spectrum. In honeycomb ferromagnets this produces topological phonon–magnon bands and a predicted thermal Hall response; related work treats magnetically dressed exciton-polaritons in van der Waals (anti)ferromagnets.

05

Synchrotron experiment

I lead synchrotron experiments, inelastic X-ray scattering (IXS), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and Raman, at facilities such as APS and CHESS, turning raw spectra into phonon dispersions, linewidths, and order parameters. Pairing IXS with machine-learning-accelerated first-principles modeling, for instance, exposed an anomalous rise of thermal conductivity with temperature in cubic GeTe driven by strengthening second-nearest-neighbor bonds.
